A rock chip in your windshield will not heal on its own. The question is only how fast it gets worse.

Why a rock chip in your windshield should not wait

You hear the ping on the highway and hope it is nothing. Sometimes it stays small. Often it does not.

A chip is a weak spot in the glass. Temperature swings, rough roads and slammed doors all push on it. Dirt and moisture work into the break while you wait, and that makes a clean repair harder.

Waiting also costs money. A resin repair costs a fraction of a replacement. A chip that spreads into a long crack usually means a new windshield.

What to do in the first hour

  1. Do not touch it. No picking, no pressing, no glue or nail polish.
  2. Cover it with clear tape. That keeps dirt and water out of the break.
  3. Skip the car wash. High-pressure water and a fresh chip do not mix.
  4. Warm the car slowly. A blast of heat stresses the glass. See our defroster guide.
  5. Take a photo. One close shot and one wide shot help with a quote or an insurance claim.
  6. Call us. We can often tell repair or replace from a short description.

When a chip is still a repair

A simple rule works well. A chip smaller than a quarter and a crack shorter than about six inches are good repair candidates. Location matters too, and damage in the driver's main viewing area is held to a stricter limit. The full size rules are in our chip repair or replacement guide.

Repair keeps your original glass. On cars with a camera behind the windshield, that can save a recalibration. See windshield chip and crack repair.

When it is too late for a repair

Some damage needs a new windshield. That includes a crack longer than about six inches, damage in the driver's line of sight, a break that reaches the edge, or three or more impact points. If that is your glass, see mobile windshield replacement.

Where chips come from around here

Rocks fly wherever trucks and loose gravel share the road. Drivers in our area tend to pick up chips on I-82 toward Prosser and Sunnyside, on SR 240 between Richland and Kennewick, and on US 395 through Pasco and Kennewick.

Will a rock chip fix itself?+
No. Glass does not heal. A chip stays a weak spot, and temperature swings, bumps and dirt can make it spread.
How fast do I need to fix a rock chip?+
As soon as you can. A fast repair keeps dirt and moisture out of the break and costs less than a replacement. Until then, cover it with clear tape.
Can I drive with a rock chip?+
Usually yes, if it is small and out of your line of sight. Drive gently and avoid potholes. If a crack grows or blocks your view, stop driving and call us.
What size chip can be repaired?+
A chip smaller than a quarter is usually a repair candidate. The industry standard limits a bullseye or half-moon chip to 1 inch and a star break to 3 inches.
Will a repair make the chip disappear?+
It usually looks much better, but you may see a faint mark where the impact was. A repair stops the damage from spreading. It does not make the glass look new.
