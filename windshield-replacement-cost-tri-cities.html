Windshield replacement cost depends on the vehicle, and no honest shop can quote it without knowing which one you drive. We do not post a flat price for that reason.

What sets the windshield replacement cost

Two windshields can look the same from the street and be very different parts. Here is what changes the price:

FactorWhy it moves the price
Year, make and modelThe glass is cut to fit your exact vehicle
Glass featuresRain sensor, heated wiper park, acoustic layer, camera bracket and tint band each change the part
Camera systemsCars with a forward camera need recalibration after the glass is replaced
OEM or aftermarket glassFactory-branded glass can cost more than an equal-fit alternative
InsuranceYour deductible decides how much of the bill you pay

A base windshield on an older sedan is one of the least expensive jobs. A late-model truck with a heated, acoustic, camera-equipped windshield sits at the other end. The part costs more, and the job includes recalibration. Our page on ADAS recalibration explains that step.

A chip repair costs far less

If your damage is small, repair is the cheaper job. A resin repair costs a fraction of a replacement, and it keeps your original glass. We would rather do the cheaper job when it works.

The catch is timing. A chip that spreads into a long crack usually needs a new windshield. Our chip repair guide shows the size limits.

How insurance changes what you pay

Glass damage is normally covered under comprehensive coverage. Some policies carry a separate, lower glass deductible. Washington does not require insurers to waive the deductible on glass claims, so your own policy decides.

Get the price first, then compare it to your deductible. Our guide on insurance claim or pay cash walks through it. Washington also lets you pick your own shop. See RCW 48.30.340.

How to get a firm quote

Have these ready when you call:

  • Year, make and model. Add the trim level if you know it.
  • The VIN. It is on a small plate at the base of the windshield on the driver's side, and on the sticker in the driver's door jamb.
  • Windshield features. Look for a camera housing near the mirror, a rain sensor, or a heated edge.
  • A photo of the damage. One close shot and one wide shot.
  • Your insurance details. Have the card handy if you plan to file.

Questions to ask any shop before you book

  • Does the quote include recalibration if my car has a camera?
  • Is the glass OEM or aftermarket, and what is the price of each?
  • What is the warranty on workmanship?
  • What is the safe drive-away time? See our drive-away guide.

How much does windshield replacement cost?+
It depends on your vehicle and the features in the glass, so we do not post a flat number. Call with your year, make and model and we will quote it for free before any work starts.
Why does the price change so much from car to car?+
The glass is made for your exact vehicle. Rain sensors, heating, acoustic layers and camera brackets all change the part, and camera cars need recalibration on top.
Is a chip repair cheaper than a replacement?+
Yes. A resin repair costs a fraction of a replacement. Fix a chip early, because one that spreads may need a new windshield.
Will insurance cover my windshield?+
Glass is normally covered under comprehensive coverage. Washington does not require a zero deductible, so check your policy for a glass deductible. Our insurance guide covers the steps.
Is OEM glass worth paying extra for?+
That depends on your vehicle and your insurer. Some require OEM-branded glass. Tell us when you call and we will price both so you can compare.
