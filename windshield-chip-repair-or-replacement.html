A small chip in the glass does not always mean a new windshield. The right answer depends on size, type, and location, and this guide walks through each one.

Windshield chip repair or replacement: the size rules

Ever watch a tiny chip turn into a crack in one cold morning? That is why size matters so much. The glass industry has a written repair standard, called the ANSI standard for repair of laminated auto glass, and it sets the outer limits.

Type of damageStandard's limit
Bullseye or half-moon chipUp to 1 inch across
Star breakUp to 3 inches across
Combination breakBody up to 2 inches across
Single crackUp to 14 inches long

Those are the outer limits. Most technicians work well inside them. A simple rule: a chip smaller than a quarter and a crack shorter than about six inches are good repair candidates.

Where the damage sits changes the answer

Two chips of the same size can need different fixes. A chip low on the glass is easy to treat. The same chip right in front of the driver is a different story.

A repair leaves a small mark where the resin filled the break. That is fine at the bottom of the glass. In the driver's primary viewing area, the standard is stricter, and it caps repairable damage at one inch there. It also says repairs should not sit within four inches of each other.

Damage that reaches the edge of the glass is another red flag. The edge is where the glass bonds to the car body, so a crack there tends to keep growing. Replacement is the usual answer.

What a repair does, and what it does not

Resin is injected into the break. It cures, seals the damage, and stops it from spreading. The chip usually looks much better afterward.

It will not look like new glass. You may still see a faint mark where the impact was, and that is normal. A repair is about stopping the damage cheaply, not making the glass perfect.

The standard also recommends replacement when there are three or more long cracks coming from one impact point. It says the same when the break holds visible dirt that cleaning cannot remove. This is why a fast call helps. The standard itself is public if you want to read it.

How to decide in four steps

  • Measure it. Compare the break to a quarter. Count the cracks coming off it.
  • Check the spot. Is it in the driver's view or touching the edge? Either one leans toward replacement.
  • Protect it today. Put clear tape over the chip. Keep water and dirt out.
  • Call and describe it. A technician can usually tell you repair or replace from a clear description and a photo.

Cost often decides the rest. A repair is far cheaper than a new windshield, and some insurers waive the deductible for a repair. Our claim or cash guide covers that decision. You can also read about our chip and crack repair service and windshield replacement service.

How big of a chip can be repaired?+
The industry repair standard allows a chip or bullseye up to about one inch across. A quarter is just under an inch. Many shops use stricter limits, and the final call depends on the type of break, where it sits, and how clean it is.
Can a long crack be repaired?+
Sometimes. The standard allows cracks up to 14 inches in some cases, but many shops stop well short of that. Cracks that reach the edge of the glass, or that run through the driver's view, usually mean a new windshield.
Will a repaired chip spread later?+
A good repair stops the break from spreading. The repair is not invisible, and the glass is not brand new. If new damage starts nearby, or the same spot cracks again, the windshield may need replacing.
Should I wait to fix a chip?+
No. Dirt and water get into the break, and the standard lists contamination that cannot be cleaned out as a reason to replace. Temperature swings can also push a chip into a crack. Cover it with clear tape and call soon.
