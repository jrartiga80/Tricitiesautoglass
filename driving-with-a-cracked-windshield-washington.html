Driving With a Cracked Windshield in Washington

What the state law says, what it leaves out, and why the crack matters more than the ticket.

Driving with a cracked windshield in Washington does not run into a simple crack-size rule. That does not make it safe.

What Washington law says about a cracked windshield

We read RCW 46.37.410, the state's windshield law. It does three things:

  • It requires a front windshield made of safety glazing material.
  • It bars signs, posters and other nontransparent material on the windshield and windows that block the driver's clear view.
  • It requires working wipers, kept in good order.

The statute does not mention cracks. We could not find a Washington law that sets a crack size for passenger cars. We are not lawyers, so for a ticket question, ask the Washington State Patrol or an attorney.

Why safety matters more than the ticket

A windshield is part of the safety system. It supports the roof in a rollover. It is also the backstop the passenger airbag inflates against.

The glass has to stay bonded in a crash. I-CAR, the collision repair training group, explains that windshield adhesive has to meet federal standards for windshield retention and occupant protection. A crack can weaken the glass. A long one can weaken it more.

When a crack means replace now

We replace rather than repair when any of these is true:

  • The crack is longer than about six inches.
  • The damage sits in the driver's primary line of sight.
  • The break has reached the edge of the glass.
  • There are three or more separate impact points.

If a crack blocks your view, do not keep driving on it. Call us and we come to you. Read how mobile windshield replacement works.

Until you get it fixed

  1. Tape a small chip. Clear tape keeps dirt and water out.
  2. Warm the car slowly. A blast of defroster heat can make a crack run. See our defroster guide.
  3. Drive gently. Skip potholes and close the doors softly.
  4. Do not stick anything on the glass. Mounts and stickers can block your view and add stress.
  5. Take photos. They help with a quote or a claim.

What it costs to fix

A short crack or chip may be a repair, and a repair costs a fraction of a replacement. A long crack usually means new glass. Glass damage is normally covered under comprehensive coverage, so read our guide on insurance claim or pay cash before you file.

Is it illegal to drive with a cracked windshield in Washington?+
RCW 46.37.410 requires safety glazing, a clear view and working wipers. It does not mention cracks, and we could not find a state crack-size rule for passenger cars. For a ticket question, ask the Washington State Patrol or an attorney.
Is a cracked windshield unsafe even if it is legal?+
It can be. The windshield supports the roof in a rollover and backs up the passenger airbag. A crack can weaken the glass, and one in your line of sight can block your view.
Can I drive to the shop with a cracked windshield?+
A short, careful drive with a small crack is different from a daily commute. If the crack blocks your view or keeps growing, do not drive. We come to you instead.
How long can I wait before fixing a crack?+
Not long. Temperature swings, bumps and dirt make cracks grow. A fast fix costs less and may still be a repair.
Will insurance cover a cracked windshield?+
Glass damage is normally covered under comprehensive coverage. Your deductible, and any separate glass deductible, decides what you pay.
