ADAS Recalibration After a Windshield Replacement

If your car has a camera behind the glass, a new windshield is only half the job.

ADAS recalibration after a windshield replacement means re-aiming the camera behind your glass so your safety features see the road correctly. Many newer cars need it.

What ADAS means

ADAS stands for advanced driver assistance systems. That covers things like lane keeping, adaptive cruise control and automatic emergency braking.

Many of these features use a camera mounted on the windshield near the mirror. The camera is aimed at the factory, based on where the glass sits.

Why a new windshield moves the camera

When the glass comes out, the camera reference changes. Even a clean install can leave the camera aimed a little differently. A small angle error at the lens turns into a bigger error down the road.

The risk is that your car may not warn you. The dash can look normal while a feature reads the road wrong. That is why a missing warning light is not proof that all is well.

How to tell if your car has a windshield camera

  • Look for a camera housing behind the rearview mirror.
  • Check for lane departure warning, lane keeping assist or adaptive cruise control.
  • Check for automatic emergency braking.

If you see any of these, plan on recalibration and confirm it with your installer. We look it up by VIN. Our page on ADAS windshield recalibration has the full details.

Static, dynamic and dual calibration

Your car maker decides which method is needed. There are three types.

  • Static: done indoors with a target board set at a measured distance.
  • Dynamic: done on the road at a set speed on clearly marked lanes.
  • Dual: a static step first, then a road drive.

The method changes the time and the setup, so ask which one your car needs.

What to ask before you book

  1. Does this quote include recalibration?
  2. Do you scan the car before and after?
  3. Will I get a report that shows the results?
  4. Is the calibration done by you, or sent out?

Keep the report. If you ever file a claim, it shows the safety systems were set to spec after the glass work.

Does a chip repair avoid recalibration?

A repair keeps your original glass in place. That usually means the camera stays where it is. This is one more reason to fix a rock chip early.

Does insurance cover recalibration?

It is often handled as part of the glass claim, because it is a required step on many cars. Check with your insurer. Read our insurance claim or pay cash guide for how to compare.

Does every car need recalibration after a new windshield?

No. Cars without a windshield camera may not need it. Most newer cars have one, so check with your installer. We look it up by VIN.

Can I skip recalibration if no warning light is on?

No. A camera that is aimed wrong may not set a warning. The dash can look normal while a feature reads the road wrong.

How long does recalibration take?

It depends on the car and the method. Static, dynamic and dual calibrations take different amounts of time. Ask for the time when you get your quote.

Is recalibration included in the quote?

It should be on a car that needs it. Ask before you book, so there are no surprises. We list it on the invoice.

What if my glass was replaced elsewhere and never recalibrated?

Call and ask. We can check your vehicle by VIN and tell you what it needs.
