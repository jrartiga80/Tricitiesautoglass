A windshield insurance claim is not always the cheapest route. Sometimes the best move is to pay cash, and the numbers tell you which.

How a windshield insurance claim works

Glass damage is normally covered under comprehensive coverage, not collision. If you carry comprehensive, a rock on the highway or a break-in is usually covered. Your deductible is the amount you pay first.

Two details can change your cost. Some policies carry a separate, lower glass deductible. Many carriers also waive the deductible for a chip repair, because repair is cheaper than the replacement it prevents. Read your policy or ask your agent to find out which applies to you.

When to file and when to pay cash

The math is short. Get the price of the fix first, then compare it to your deductible.

Your situationUsual best move
Chip repair, deductible waivedFile. It may cost you nothing.
Repair costs less than deductible, no waiverPay cash. It is simpler.
Replacement costs more than deductibleFile. Insurance pays the difference.
Replacement with a camera systemAsk about recalibration coverage first.

Do not guess at the repair price. A quick call with a clear description is free, and it keeps you from filing a claim you did not need.

Will it affect your rates?

Most carriers treat comprehensive glass claims as not-at-fault. That generally means they do not hit your premium the way a collision does. Frequency can still matter, so several claims in a short time may come up at renewal.

We are not insurance agents. Your agent is the right person for anything about your policy or your rates. We can tell you what the repair costs and what the replacement costs.

Steps to take before you file

  • Photograph the damage. Take one close shot and one wide shot.
  • Get the repair or replace answer. Our chip repair guide shows how to tell.
  • Find your deductible. Look for a separate glass deductible on your policy.
  • Ask about shop choice. Confirm you can pick your own shop.
  • Keep the paperwork. Save the invoice and any recalibration records.

Should I file a claim for a windshield chip?+
Compare the repair price to your deductible first. Many insurers waive the deductible for a chip repair, so it may cost you nothing. If the repair costs less than your deductible and there is no waiver, paying cash is usually simpler.
Does a windshield claim raise my rates?+
Glass claims under comprehensive coverage are usually treated as not-at-fault, so they generally do not raise rates like a collision claim. Several claims in a short time can come up at renewal. Ask your agent, because rates are set by your carrier.
Do I have to use my insurer's glass shop?+
You can usually choose your own shop. Insurers often recommend a network shop, but the choice is typically yours. Ask your insurer to confirm how it works on your policy before you book.
What do I need to file a glass claim?+
Have your carrier name, your policy number, and photos of the damage ready. The claim number comes once the claim is open. Keep the invoice and service records after the work is done.
