Windshield Replacement Aftercare: The First Days

The glass is in. Here is how to help the adhesive do its job.

Windshield replacement aftercare is mostly about patience. Your installer's instructions come first, because they depend on the adhesive and your vehicle.

What follows is common advice. If anything here conflicts with what your installer told you, follow your installer.

Wait for the safe drive-away time

Do not drive before your installer says it is safe. The adhesive needs time to build strength. Read our guide on safe drive-away time for why it varies.

Leave any tape on

Installers sometimes put tape on the glass to hold the trim in place. Leave it until your installer says it can come off. Do not pull on it.

Go easy on the doors

Slamming a door pushes air against the new glass. Close doors gently for the first day or so. Cracking a window slightly can also help, since it lets pressure out.

Skip the car wash for a bit

Ask your installer how long to wait. High-pressure water and brush washes are the usual things to avoid early on.

Watch for leaks and noise

Check for water at the edges after rain. Listen for wind noise or whistling on the highway. Check the trim too. If something is off, tell your installer right away. Warranty claims go smoother when you speak up early.

Be careful with heat on cold mornings

Do not blast the defroster at full heat on frozen glass, and never pour hot water on it. Warm the car gradually. See our defroster guide and our guide on hot water on frozen windshields.

Keep the paperwork

Keep your invoice and warranty. If your car has a camera, keep the recalibration report too. See ADAS recalibration after a windshield replacement.

When to call your installer

  • You see water inside the car after rain.
  • You hear a whistle or wind noise.
  • The trim looks loose or lifted.
  • A dash warning appears after the job.

When can I wash my car after a windshield replacement?

Ask your installer. Many shops suggest waiting a short while and avoiding high-pressure washes at first.

Can I slam the car door after a new windshield?

Try not to for the first day or so. A slammed door pushes air against fresh adhesive. Close doors gently.

Should I leave the tape on?

Yes, until your installer says it can come off. It holds the trim while the adhesive sets.

Is a little wind noise normal?

Not usually. Wind noise or whistling can mean a gap in the seal. Call your installer so they can check it.

Can I turn on the defroster?

Yes, but warm the car gradually. A sudden blast of heat on very cold glass puts stress on it.
