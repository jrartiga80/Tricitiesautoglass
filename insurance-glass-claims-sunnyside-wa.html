What your policy probably covers

Glass claims are paid under comprehensive coverage, not liability or collision. If you carry comprehensive, damage from a rock on the highway, vandalism, or a break-in is normally covered.

Washington is not a zero-deductible glass state — insurers here are not required to waive your deductible on a windshield replacement. But two things frequently work in your favor: many policies carry a separate, lower glass deductible, and many carriers waive the deductible entirely for a chip repair because it is far cheaper than the replacement it prevents. That is why we always check repair eligibility first.

You choose who does the work

Insurers maintain networks and will often steer you toward one. In Washington you have the right to choose your own glass shop, and choosing a local one does not change your coverage or your deductible.

We are happy to work inside the network process. We take the claim number, submit the invoice, document the parts and the ADAS recalibration, and follow up on payment ourselves.

Does Washington waive the deductible for windshield replacement?+
No. Washington does not require insurers to waive glass deductibles, unlike a handful of other states. Many policies do carry a separate lower glass deductible, and many carriers waive it for a chip repair, so it is always worth checking before you pay out of pocket.
